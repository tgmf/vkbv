# 06 — Interface

The interface has one job: make cause and effect across time readable, including
what is still open. When in doubt, prefer the clearer option over the prettier one.

The world view is an orthographic three.js scene behind the `SceneRenderer` interface
(`02` §4). Panels, hand, timeline and overlays are Solid 2.0 components over it.

## 1. Layout

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┬───────────────┐
│ top bar: town name · clock (HH:MM) · settings · language      │  side panel   │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤  ─ charge     │
│                                                              │  ─ powers     │
│                world view (three.js canvas)                  │  ─ journal    │
│                                                              │    hooks      │
│                                                              │    stories    │
│                                                              │  ─ line log   │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤               │
│ hand of cards                                                │               │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┴───────────────┤
│ timeline: ▶ ⏸ speed │ tracks                                                 │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The timeline is always visible, spans the full width, and is at least 120 px tall. It
is the most important control in the game.

## 2. World view (graybox)

**Scene.** One orthographic three.js scene in the final camera: a fixed 3/4 view
from above, the three regions laid out by their `pos` in metres (x east, z south). The
camera is free: pan by drag, zoom by wheel or pinch within limits, and "follow Marti"
(on by default) keeps him in view. **The camera never changes the wave.** Panning
anywhere shows possibilities, never facts.

**Places.** One flat box per node, sized and coloured by tags (indoor, outdoor,
shore); edges as paths on the ground. The **fog** is one thing (Tagir, 2026-11-25,
`docs/questions.md` M5-5): everything outside the lanterns, drawn **blurred**, places and
ghosts alike (the town runs as blurred ghosts while Marti is home). A place not yet
revealed is in the fog like any other; it only keeps its name and objects hidden and
cannot be targeted until it is revealed.

**Cats.** Each cat is a flat cut-out billboard (a rounded silhouette with ears) in
its `look` colours, always facing the camera. Protagonists carry their name.

**The lantern.** Around each followed protagonist, the area he observes: a
disc of his current sight range (`03` §4, including rain and walls), an even tint
inside a crisp outline. It is not a light source and the area does not fade; the outline
may carry a small decorative fade, as a vision circle in a real-time strategy game
(Tagir, 2026-11-25). Inside it the world is crisp; it shrinks in the rain. Indoors, where
walls stop sight, it is the circle around the places he sees (his flat), not a disc
reaching through the walls. A second lantern appears only when a second protagonist
is followed (not in the demo).

**Ghosts.** For every cat, the frame at the playhead gives its distinct
`(position, activity)` with probabilities (`03` §16). Each is drawn as a see-through
copy of the cat with opacity proportional to its probability and additive glow, so
likely ghosts read solid and rare ones faint. Overlapping ghosts at one place cluster
into one marker. Cats Marti has watched this tick are drawn solid inside the lantern.
Exact probabilities are never shown outside the debug panel.

**Focus layer** (setting, on by default): on top of the ghosts, one coherent trajectory
from the frame's `samples` (`03` §16), chosen in turn, switches every
`1 / render.focusLayerHz` seconds (at most 3 Hz) with a short crossfade. Its random
choice uses a render-only seeded generator, never the simulation's `rand` and never
`Math.random`. With reduced motion, the focus layer is off and ghosts use static
opacity.

**Display budget.** At rest the view draws at most `render.displayBudget` cat
markers, ranked by salience: followed protagonists, then cats within the lantern,
then cats targeted by a held card, then by probability mass near the camera centre.
Others are folded into their place's cluster count.

**Position** between ticks is interpolated along the edge from `left / of` and the
playback fraction; floats live only in `render` and `ui`.

**Lines.** When a watched fact contains a storylet state with a `textKey`, or a
`voice` of the followed cat, a speech bubble appears over the cat (or place) for
about 4 real seconds and the line goes into the side panel's line log. Drive keys
(`hooks[].driveKey` text) are highlighted inside voiced lines. Scrubbing shows only
bubbles for the tick under the playhead. Lines of storylets Marti does not perceive are
never shown.

**Objects** are small icons at their node, pulsing gently until inspected. An object
with `visibleWhen` is drawn with opacity = the probability it is visible.

**World state.** Rain: an animated overlay on outdoor places with opacity = P(rain)
at the playhead. Closed edges: a barrier across the edge with opacity = P(closed). A
running lure: a soft glowing cat silhouette at its place. A Gaze: a faint ring of its
radius for its ticks.

**Assist mode** (setting `flags.assist`): highlights on places and cats for live
partial matches of the anchor's requirement tree, of paid events, and of authored
storylets not yet watched, with opacity = match probability. Off by default.

## 3. Timeline

Tracks, top to bottom:

1. **Clock ruler:** hour marks from 05:40 to the lantern edge.
2. **Weather:** a band whose opacity is P(rain) at each tick; solid once watched.
3. **Story:** a dot at the tick of each watched storylet state or detector that has a
   payout or a text line. Unwatched story is never shown. The anchor, once watched, is
   a lightning mark.
4. **Pins:** one marker per pin, with the card or power icon. A pin that fizzled
   everywhere is hollow; one that fizzled with some probability is half-filled; both
   explain why on hover or focus.
5. **Presence strip** (only while a card or power is held and the pointer is over a
   place or cat): shades the ticks at which a valid target is at that spot, darker
   where more likely, so the player sees *when* to act there.

Overlays:

- **Playhead:** a vertical line with a handle and the time.
- **The lantern edge** is the bar's right end: nothing past it is drawn, and the
  playhead cannot pass it. The bar spans `max(lanternEdge, timeline.minSpanTicks)`
  ticks, so it grows freely at first and then rescales to keep the edge on the right.
  Live playback at the edge pushes it along.
- **Re-run:** after an edit the bar refills from the edited tick as the re-run streams
  in (`02` §3), like a video buffering; the playhead can move only through what has
  arrived.
- **Fray:** when the timeline is frayed, the bar ends at the fray tick with the last
  stretch of the tracks drawn broken and fading.
- **Changed, unwatched:** a small open circle at each tick in
  `marks.changedUnwatched`, cleared when that tick is watched again.

**The shuttle** (decided 2026-11-22, a VHS shuttle; `04` §6):

- Clicking or dragging the timeline moves the playhead only to ticks at or before the
  playhead: nothing after it is clickable. Dragging back scrubs: the view blurs slightly
  and nothing is observed. There is no "jump to the edge" by default; `flags.jumpToEdge`
  adds one (a button and End, moving the playhead to the lantern edge as a scrub) for
  playtests only.
- Forward is play or the shuttle; back is the shuttle. The shuttle runs at
  `player.shuttleTps` ticks per real second (4, 16, 64, 256) in either direction: each
  press of forward or back steps up one speed in that direction (from the other
  direction it starts again at the first), and pause stops. Play runs forward at the
  first speed.
- Backward at any speed is scrubbing: it observes nothing.
- Forward at up to `player.observeMaxTps` (16) is playback: it observes, records and pays
  (`04` §7). Faster forward behind the lantern edge is a scrub and observes nothing.
- After an edit the re-run streams in from the edit tick (`02` §3): forward can't pass the
  last tick that has arrived; the playhead waits there and follows the stream.
- Past the lantern edge, forward is always live playback: capped at
  `player.observeMaxTps` and at what the simulation sustains, and the edge moves with it.
  It stops by itself at the fray and at the end.

Pins: drag horizontally to move (`movePin`), a small × on hover or focus, or Delete
when focused, withdraws. Unique pins show a lock and cannot be dragged or withdrawn.

## 4. Hand and playing a card

- Cards in zone `hand` show name, one-line text and a target hint (cat, object,
  place).
- Click or tap a card to hold it, or drag it. While held: valid targets are ringed and
  everything else dims (validity comes from `src/player`, never from the UI); the
  **magnifying ring** spreads clustered cats and enlarges those the card can affect,
  ghosts included; the presence strip is active.
- Click a target (or drop on it) to pin at the playhead. Escape or empty space
  cancels.
- After a pin, the edit preview runs (§6); then the edit lands and edit feedback runs.
- Rule `discard` shows draw and discard counts by the hand.

## 5. Powers

The side panel shows the charge meter (no number outside debug; a bar with the
powers' costs marked on it) and one button per power: **Lure** and **Gaze**. A power
button is disabled, with the reason, when charge is short. Holding a power works like
holding a card, targeting revealed places. Gaze shows its radius ring under the
pointer.

## 6. Inspecting, specials, edits

- Clicking an object with nothing held inspects it: a panel shows its name and story
  and, the first time, the card it gives, which then flies to the hand.
- The journal lists discovered hooks (name and the line that revealed them) and
  inspected objects.
- **Special confirm:** pinning a card on its own source object the first time shows a
  confirm step in place: the special's text and "Once per playthrough. This cannot be
  taken back." with Confirm and Cancel.
- **Break alert:** when an edit preview reports paid facts that would reopen, or would
  move unique or power pins to the new edge (`04` §4), a bar slides in above the
  timeline before the edit lands: "This change undoes:" followed by the watched lines it
  would undo and each pin that would move ("Melman's whistle moves to 09:29"), with
  Confirm and Cancel. It is
  information, not a penalty. Not a modal: the rest of the screen stays visible, but
  the edit waits for the choice.

**Edit feedback**, after every edit that lands:

1. Story dots that appear, disappear or move pulse for about 2 seconds.
2. A **ripple** travels along the timeline from the edited tick to the right: it is
   the re-run streaming in.
3. In the world view, Marti's **previous path** (his path before the edit, from the
   old frames) is drawn as a faint line for about 5 seconds where it differs from the
   new one. Never call it a ghost in the interface.
4. The bar's end moves to the new lantern edge and the bar rescales; if the end changed
   (to or from fray or anchor), it animates to its new state.
5. Pins the edit leaves past the new edge (`04` §4): ordinary cards fly back to the
   hand; unique and power pins slide to the edge.

This feedback is the core of the game. Do not skip it or reduce it to a toast.

## 7. Teaching without a tutorial

Hints are one short line near the thing they refer to. At most one is visible. Each
shows once per playthrough, can be dismissed, and never blocks input. Hints describe
controls and mechanics only; they never tell the story or judge the player.

| Hint | Shown when | English text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `watch` | game start, after the teaser | Watch. Space pauses. |
| `ghosts` | 30 real seconds into playback | What no one has watched is still open. |
| `inspect` | 20 real seconds without an inspect | Look closer at things in the flat. Each one remembers something. |
| `card` | first card reaches the hand | Hold a card, then pick a cat, a thing or a place. |
| `barely` | first card pinned on Marti, 2 seconds later | He hardly noticed. Something else did. (charge meter pulses) |
| `scroll` | three cards found, or the playhead past 08:40 | Drag the timeline back. Nothing you haven't seen is settled yet. |
| `fray` | the playhead first reaches a fray | From here on, the meeting can't happen. Something earlier has to change. |
| `power` | charge first reaches the lure's cost | You can show yourself now. (Lure pulses) |
| `notready` | first watched `door_lure.back_inside` | He is not ready. What does he want right now? (journal pulses) |
| `morning` | first watched `usual_route.shop_open` or `usual_route.shop_closed` | The road was open. Was it always? Go back to the morning. |
| `others` | first card held while a revealed non-protagonist cat is in view | Other cats listen more easily. |
| `gaze` | charge first reaches Gaze's cost after a lure was placed | You can fix a small part of the world in place. (Gaze pulses) |
| `sandbox` | the end screen closed | The day is still yours. Try another way. |

Hint text lives in `ui/en.json` and `ui/ru.json`; write the Russian as plain
interface text for Tagir to review. Where the slice already has a hint line
(`ui.hint.inspect`, `ui.hint.scroll`, `ui.hint.fray`), use that key.

## 8. Keyboard and accessibility

| Key | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Space | play / pause |
| J / K / L | shuttle back / pause / shuttle forward; press J or L again to go faster |
| ← | one tick back (a scrub) |
| → | one tick forward as playback |
| Home | jump to tick 0 |
| End | jump to the lantern edge, only with `flags.jumpToEdge` (a playtest option) |
| 1 to 9 | hold the nth card in hand |
| G | hold Gaze (L is the shuttle; the lure is held from its button, `docs/questions.md` M5-4) |
| Tab / Shift+Tab | move focus through valid targets while holding; through pins otherwise |
| Enter | pin on the focused target; confirm a confirm step |
| Delete | withdraw the focused pin |
| Escape | cancel a hold, close a panel, cancel a confirm step |
| ` | debug panel |

- Every control is reachable by keyboard and has an accessible name. Cats (ghosts as
  one entry per cat), places and objects are focusable while something is held, via
  an accessible list mirrored from the scene.
- Never colour alone: fizzled pins are hollow or half-filled, valid targets have an
  outline, unique pins a lock.
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: no ripple animation, no pulsing, no focus layer; state
  changes are still shown.
- Text never smaller than 14 px; contrast at least 4.5:1; a text-size setting (100%,
  125%, 150%).
- Settings: language, text size, reduced-motion override, focus layer, assist mode,
  sound volume (unused until M9), new game (with confirm).

## 9. Teaser, strike, end screen, locked thread

- **Teaser:** on a new game, before tick 0, a full-view card for a few seconds: the
  beach, the boat, rain, two cat silhouettes, the anchor's `teaserKey` line. Any key
  or click skips it. In the graybox it is a composition of the scene's own shapes.
- **Strike:** when playback watches the anchor, the lightning mark lands on the
  timeline and the view holds on the boat for the remaining ticks.
- **End screen:** title, one line (`[[TODO writer]]`), three buttons with URLs from
  `VITE_URL_FOLLOW`, `VITE_URL_WISHLIST`, `VITE_URL_NEWSLETTER` (a button without a
  URL is hidden), and "Keep playing", which closes it and leaves the timeline open.
- **Locked thread:** after the anchor, Rokko's thread appears as a second, greyed
  track under the timeline with a lock and `ui.end.locked_rokko` on hover.

## 10. Debug panel

Behind `?debug=1` or the backquote key. Tabs:

- **Tuning:** every key of `tuning.json` as an editable field, grouped. Changes
  recompute at once. Buttons: reset, copy as JSON, paste JSON.
- **Wave:** per tick (small charts) the number of cases, live cats and fog states;
  strain, re-run time of the last edit, `localCauses`, the cases at the playhead
  with their links and weights, reopened facts of the last recompute.
- **State:** for the tick under the playhead, the lantern state (every live cat's
  position, activity, interrupt, gauges, pressure, `touchedBy`; facts, flags,
  counters, storylet states, pools) and, for a picked fogged cat, its chain per case.
- **Events:** the lantern's events, filterable by type; click to jump.
- **Player:** the raw `PlayerState`; buttons: reveal all, unlock all cards (including
  test cards), +100 charge, clear `paid`, clear `watched`, switch `playedCardRule`.
- **Pin sets:** load any pin set from `05` §5 into the log.
- **Telemetry:** the session's event buffer and an export button.

## 11. Text

All player-visible text goes through `t(key)`: interface strings from `ui/*.json`,
content strings from `ch1/text/*.json`. Missing keys render as `⟦key⟧` and log once.
English is the source locale. Russian is complete from the first milestone that adds
a string.

## 12. What the interface must never do

- Decide a game rule. Validity, costs, limits and rewards come from `src/player`.
- Show a gauge value, a probability, a weight or a score outside the debug panel.
- Let the camera, panning, zoom or scrubbing collapse anything.
- Block the screen with a modal dialog.
- Mention saves, games, players or screens in any text a character speaks.
